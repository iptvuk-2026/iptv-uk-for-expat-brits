Why So Many Brits Abroad Still Want UK Television

Ask a Brit who has lived abroad for a year what they miss most and the answer is rarely the weather. It is the small, everyday things: the morning radio voices, the newsreader they grew up with, the Saturday night show, the drama everyone back home will discuss on Monday. Television is the background noise of home, and without it the distance feels longer.

Children abroad pick up local accents within months, and many parents want them to keep a connection with British programmes and voices. Retirees in the sun want to keep the evening routine exactly as it was. But UK television does not travel with you: the streaming and catch-up apps that work in Leeds usually stop working abroad, because programmes are licensed country by country. An IPTV UK subscription solves this by delivering live UK channels and on-demand programmes over the internet, on the devices you already own, wherever you live.

Second comes live sport — for many Brits abroad, the non-negotiable part of the subscription. Sport is where a weak service is exposed fastest, because a live match cannot be rewatched: a freeze during a goal is gone for good. Where in the World Are You Watching From?

Brits live everywhere, and where you live changes what matters. Here is what to expect where most expat Brits settle.

Spain & Portugal

EU • 1 hr ahead

The classic retirement move. Coastal broadband is generally good, and the one-hour gap puts UK prime time at a comfortable 8:30pm or 9:30pm.

Dubai & the Gulf

UAE • 3–4 hrs ahead

Fast fibre in the cities, but the three- to four-hour gap pushes UK prime time to late evening, so catch-up matters more here than anywhere in Europe. Many viewers use a VPN daily.

Australia & New Zealand

AU/NZ • 9–13 hrs ahead

The hardest time zone in the expat world. UK evening television airs in the small hours, so the EPG and catch-up carry the whole service.

USA & Canada

NA • 5–8 hrs behind

British daytime becomes North American morning viewing, and UK prime time lands in the afternoon. A good on-demand library lets you watch the evening's programmes in your evening instead.

Whatever your time zone, the lesson is the same: the further you are from the UK, the more the guide, catch-up and on-demand library matter. Live television is only half the product abroad; the other half is watching what you want, when you want it.

Internet Speed, VPNs and Geo-Restrictions

Internet speed first. HD needs a steady connection rather than a spectacular one: a stable 10–15 Mbps is generally enough, with more headroom helping for 4K or several viewers at once. What matters is the speed you actually get in the evening, not the advertised figure — test at 8pm local time. Our internet speed guide explains the figures, and if the picture freezes, our freezing fix page walks through the checks.

VPNs second. A virtual private network routes your connection through a server in another country, which can help when a local provider restricts streaming or a service checks your location — many expats in the Gulf and parts of Asia use one daily. But a VPN adds a step to the stream's journey, and a slow VPN server will cause the very buffering it was meant to fix. Choose a server close to the UK and test with it on and off.

Geo-restrictions third. A UK catch-up app stops working abroad because of licensing, not technology: the broadcaster only bought the rights to show that programme in the UK. An IPTV service is different — you watch the provider's stream, not the broadcaster's app — but that only helps if the provider serves viewers abroad. Ask before you pay: does it support viewers outside the UK, and in your country specifically? Our guide to whether IPTV is legal in the UK is worth reading, as is our page on IPTV versus Sky.

Expat IPTV FAQs

Can I watch UK TV abroad with IPTV?

Yes — an IPTV UK subscription delivers UK channels over the internet wherever you live, provided the provider supports viewers outside the UK.

Do I need a VPN to watch IPTV abroad?

Not always. A VPN helps where a local provider restricts streaming, but it can slow the stream, so test with it on and off.

Will the TV guide show my local time?

It should, once the app is set to your time zone. If the times look wrong, refresh the guide data — our EPG not updating page explains how.

Can I use one subscription on several devices abroad?

Yes — one login typically covers the television, phone, tablet and laptop in your household. Check the pricing page for how many screens your plan allows.
